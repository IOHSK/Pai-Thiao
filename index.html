Seven stretches

Your time in Bangkok, from landing at Suvarnabhumi to heading back to the airport, split into seven stretches across the city. Each one teaches what you will really run into in that part of town, and every lesson you finish earns a stamp in your passport.

    Travel companions

    Three Thai friends take turns helping you along the way.

    Mali, the Siamese cat

    Named after the jasmine flower, mà-lí. Sharp ears, so she looks after pronunciation and tones.

    Chang, the elephant

    Thailand's national animal. Patient and never in a hurry; when you miss something, he tells you why.

    Tukkae, the gecko

    The tokay gecko is named for its own call, "tuk-kae!". He clings to every wall in town and brings you stories about each stop.

    My passport

    Finish a lesson and get a stamp. The lotus petals show how many points you earned, up to three.

    How it works

    One lesson a day, ten minutes

    Each lesson goes: listen, pick what you heard, say it, complete the phrase, get your stamp. If you did not earn all three lotuses, ride the same lesson again tomorrow.

    Your polite ending

    Thai speakers end most sentences with khráp (men) or khâ (women). Tap the yellow button at the top to choose yours. Every phrase on the site, and the word for "I", switches with it.

    Reading the romanization

    Thai script appears with a romanization that marks the tone: à low, â falling, á high, ǎ rising, no mark for mid. In Korean view you also get a Hangul hint, but trust your ears over the letters.

    The Thai of Bangkok

    Lessons follow standard central Thai. Sounds come from your device's Thai (th-TH) voice; recorded voices will be added over time. A slow button in every lesson helps with tones.

    Speaking practice works best in Chrome

    Checking what you say works best in Chrome, Edge, and Chrome on Android. Machine ears struggle with tones, so treat it as a friendly check, not a judge.

    No sign-up, no ads

    Progress is saved only in this browser on this device. If you switch devices, your progress stays behind.
